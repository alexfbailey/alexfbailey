TWEED
Wool · Traditional Weave · British Isles

A rough, unfinished woollen cloth of a soft, open, flexible texture, typically featuring flecks of mixed colour. Originally hand-woven on the islands and highlands of Scotland and Ireland, tweed is renowned for its durability and warmth in outdoor conditions.

Story

The name tweed is thought to derive from a misreading of the Scottish word tweel (twill) in a London merchant's letter, c. 1830. Harris Tweed, produced in the Outer Hebrides, remains the only textile in the world with its own act of Parliament protecting its authenticity and handmade origins.

  • Trousers, skirts & waistcoats
